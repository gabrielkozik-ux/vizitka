Claude vs. Gemini vs. ChatGPT: Which AI Tool Should a Freelancer or Small Business Choose?

Published: July 30, 2026 | Author: Mgr. Gabriel Kožík

A comparison of three assistants from the perspective of someone who uses them in legal practice — and who also deals with what happens to your data afterwards.

Prices and model names change within weeks, so verify them before making a decision. The legal and security part of this article, however, holds regardless of which model version is currently running. Correct as of 30 July 2026.

One more thing, so you read this in the right light: alongside my legal practice, I train companies in working with Google's AI tools. Please read the recommendations in this article with that in mind — I aim to be fair, but I am not a disinterested observer.

Three colleagues you could hire

Before we get to tables and prices, let us picture these three tools as people you might bring onto your team.

Claude is your specialist analyst

Systematic about everything, more likely to ask than to assume, and willing to admit when it is not sure. It is somewhat technical and occasionally slower — but for reviewing a contract, drafting a legal analysis or writing a long text, it is exactly the kind of colleague you want beside you.

ChatGPT is the all-rounder with the biggest network

It handles everything: it can talk, draw, generate code and connect applications. If you want one person "for everything" and do not want to think about who is best at what, this is your pick.

Gemini is the colleague already sitting in your office

It sees into your emails, documents and spreadsheets in Google Workspace, so you do not have to explain anything or upload anything anywhere. In a team environment it makes the most economic sense — and it is the only one that resolves confidentiality contractually through the business plan.

This is how the tools behave today. Development is extremely fast and the picture may look different in six months.

Beware of sycophancy

Before the technical detail, one thing that applies to all three equally and will save you the most trouble and money.

Language models have a natural tendency to agree with whatever you put in front of them. If you type "I think this contractual clause is fine, do you agree?", there is a strong chance you will get a nod — regardless of whether the clause is fine or is walking you into trouble.

So always ask the other way round:

  • "Find me the three main weak points in this draft."
  • "What would the other side or a sceptical investor argue against this?"
  • "Where in this contract am I taking the biggest risk?"

This is where the line runs between a dangerous output and material you can actually use in business.

First, get your bearings in the names

For a beginner, navigating model names is often a bigger obstacle than choosing the tool itself. Here is where things stand today.

Claude (Anthropic)

On the Pro plan (USD 20 per month), the default model is Sonnet 5 as the fast workhorse, and the strongest available model is Opus 5 for deep analysis and demanding texts. Above this line sit the Mythos-class models — but they are not generally available; Anthropic releases them only to a limited circle of partners. The publicly usable variant of that class is Fable 5. For ordinary business work you will manage without both.

Gemini (Google)

You do not pick a numbered model but a working mode: Fast for everyday operational queries and Thinking for complex analytical tasks. Underneath runs the current Gemini generation — Gemini 3.1 Pro on the Google AI Pro plan, the older Gemini 3 Pro on the cheaper AI Plus.

ChatGPT (OpenAI)

Here too you no longer pick a model but a level of reasoning. The default is Instant (powered by GPT-5.5 Instant) for fast answers, with Medium, High and Extra High above it, all running on GPT-5.6 Sol for demanding logical tasks. Labels such as Terra or Luna appear only in the API and enterprise environments.

Practical advice: do not agonise over versions. Leave the default setting and switch to the stronger analytical mode only when you are dealing with a contract review, a financial model or a strategic question.

Pricing: standalone vs. bundled

Standalone individual plans cost practically the same across all three players — around USD 20 per month (Claude Pro, ChatGPT Plus, Google AI Pro at USD 19.99). If you are looking for the cheapest individual entry point, there are basic tiers such as Google AI Plus (USD 4.99) or ChatGPT Go.

If you run a team or a small company, however, the economics change. Gemini within Google Workspace makes the most sense. In 2025 Google discontinued the separate AI add-ons and folded Gemini directly into all Workspace plans — Business Standard therefore comes to roughly USD 14 per user per month on annual billing. For that price you get not just an AI assistant but a complete business infrastructure: email on your own domain, shared cloud storage, calendar and an office suite.

The crucial part: what happens to your data

This is where companies most often go wrong. Choosing the right security regime matters far more than whether a model scores two percent better on a logic benchmark.

Tool / plan Trained on your data? Legal protection (DPA) Suitability for company data
Claude Pro / Max Yes, unless you switch it off manually in settings. With training enabled, data retention extends from 30 days to 5 years. No guaranteed DPA on a standard account. Conditional — after disabling training and anonymising.
ChatGPT Plus / Pro Yes, unless you switch it off in Data Controls. No guaranteed DPA on a standard account. Conditional — after disabling training and anonymising.
Gemini — ordinary consumer app Yes, and prompts may be read by human reviewers. No DPA in place. Not suitable
Gemini via Google Workspace No. Training is contractually excluded. A DPA is automatically part of the contract and includes standard contractual clauses. Suitable — stored data stays in the EU.

On EU data residency: with Google Workspace the EU guarantee applies primarily to stored data. The computational processing of a query itself may technically run across global infrastructure. So this is not one hundred percent "EU-only processing" — but from a compliance standpoint it is a far more robust position than any consumer version.

The risk called shadow AI

The greatest risk in small companies today is not the absence of AI tools but so-called shadow AI. In good faith, employees make their work easier by uploading client databases, internal contracts or financial statements into their private accounts. At that moment, as a director you lose control over both trade secrets and GDPR compliance.

The answer is not a ban — people will work around it. The answer is to give them a company account so they have no reason to reach for a private one, and to add clear rules on what belongs where. One tool that fits such a setup well is NotebookLM.

NotebookLM: a closed environment for your documents

If you need to work with internal policies, hundreds of pages of PDFs or recordings from meetings, try Google's NotebookLM. It draws on the power of Gemini, but you work in a closed environment over sources you upload yourself. The model does not invent things — it draws exclusively on your materials — and gives you a precise citation for every answer. For research and an internal knowledge base it is currently the strongest tool in its category.

An important distinction: NotebookLM within Google Workspace falls under the corporate DPA and your data is not used for training — sensitive documents belong here. NotebookLM on a private Google account runs in the same regime as consumer Gemini, i.e. without a DPA. The risk is significantly lower than with open chat, but it is not zero.

Anonymisation is not the same as pseudonymisation

Many entrepreneurs believe that replacing a client's name with "Client A" ticks the GDPR box. It does not.

Pseudonymisation — not enough

"Client A, the operator of the only bakery in a village of six hundred inhabitants near Tišnov, is claiming a CZK 340,000 reduction in the contract price following the oven failure on 14 March 2025."

The name is gone, but the person remains easily identifiable. In legal terms this is still personal data.

Anonymisation — this is how

"Client A, the operator of the only bakery in a village of six hundred inhabitants near Tišnov, is claiming a CZK 340,000 reduction in the contract price following the equipment failure on 14 March 2025."

The unique contextual details are removed as well — the trade combined with the size and location of the village, the exact amount and the date. Only now is the link to a specific person broken.

True anonymisation means the data cannot be traced back to a specific person by any reasonably available means, including in combination with other information. For smaller municipalities and distinctive cases, the bar is considerably higher than it appears.

A rule for practice: when working with sensitive data, always combine anonymisation (removing unique contextual details) with the use of a company account where training is contractually disabled. One without the other is a half-measure.

Where each tool falls short

To be fair, every system has its flaws. The following observations come from my own practice rather than public benchmarks — someone else's experience may differ.

  • Gemini — in my experience, somewhat weaker polish in written Czech; with stylised PR copy it can sound stilted and "translated". It also lags behind on more complex multi-step reasoning.
  • Claude — slower, the free tier hits limits within a handful of queries, and of the three it offers the fewest direct integrations into other applications.
  • ChatGPT — built as a universal generalist. It may not be the outright leader in any single discipline, and on the free plan it will bother you with advertising and strict limits.

Which tool for which task

Type of task Recommended tool Why
Contracts, legal analyses, long texts Claude Best handling of detail, precise style, consistency across a long document.
Everyday work in email, Docs and Sheets Gemini Direct integration into Google Workspace without moving anything anywhere.
Research across your own documents and PDFs with citations NotebookLM A closed environment over your own sources with accurate referencing.
Audio and video analysis Gemini Native multimodality — the model understands sound and image directly, not via transcription.
Voice conversation, image generation ChatGPT The most developed ecosystem for voice input and media generation.
A universal all-in-one tool ChatGPT The broadest range of functions in a single subscription.

My verdict for freelancers and small businesses

If, as an individual, you are primarily looking for a tool for writing, ideation and analysis of complex documents, invest in Claude Pro.

If your company uses Google Workspace, the most sensible step is to activate Gemini for Workspace. You get a solid assistant directly in your emails and documents, an environment without data training, and DPA coverage included. For more demanding legal and analytical tasks, simply add one or two Claude Pro licences for key people.
