Claude vs. Gemini vs. ChatGPT: Which AI Tool Should a Freelancer or Small Business Choose?

Published: July 30, 2026 | Author: Mgr. Gabriel Kožík

A comparison of three AI assistants from the perspective of someone who uses them in practice — and who also has to figure out how AI actually handles your data.

Prices and model names change within weeks, so verify them before making a decision. Correct as of 30 July 2026.

One transparent disclosure up front. Alongside my legal practice, I also train entrepreneurs and companies in working with Google's AI tools. Please read the recommendations in this article with that in mind. What follows is my own assessment — I try to be fair, but I am not a disinterested observer.

Three colleagues you could hire

Before we get to tables and prices, let's picture these three tools as people you might bring onto your team.

Claude is your specialist analyst

It approaches everything systematically, would rather ask than assume, and admits when it isn't sure. It's somewhat technical and occasionally slower. For analysis or contract review, quasi-expert advisory work, or writing long, technical texts, it's exactly the kind of assistant you want beside you. One drawback for some users: the interface is available only in English. And unfortunately, Claude still can't process audio input.

ChatGPT is the all-rounder with the biggest network

It handles everything: it can talk, draw, generate code and connect applications. If you want one person "for everything" and don't want to think about who's best at what, you can rely on it — a true all-rounder for everyday work as well as more technical tasks. That said, in my experience Claude is still somewhat more precise on the technical side.

Gemini is the colleague already sitting in your office

It sees into your emails, documents and spreadsheets in Google Workspace, so you don't have to explain anything or upload anything anywhere. In a team environment it makes the most economic sense. Of the three, it's the only one whose business plan offers the simplest path to GDPR compliance and contractual data-security guarantees.

Three things worth repeating. First: this is how these tools behave today — development moves extremely fast, and the picture could look different within six months. Second: output is only as good as the input (and the context) you give it, though compared to the past all models now generally produce better results. Third: never put sensitive data into consumer or free versions of AI tools — and for business licences, make sure you set proper legal and technical security policies.

Watch out for sycophancy, too

This is the alpha and omega of working with any AI chatbot.

Given how these language models are trained, they have a natural tendency to agree with whatever you put in front of them. If you type into the chat "Do you think this contract is fine for me?", you increase the chance of getting a nod regardless of where the "objective truth" actually lies. The reason is that your own prompt statistically raises the weight toward a positive answer just by including the word "fine". Words like "well written" or "appropriately worded" work the same way.

Don't be afraid to be critical:

  • "Find the weak points in this draft."
  • "What would the other side or a sceptical investor argue against this?"
  • "What here represents a risk for me, and why?"

This is exactly where the line runs between a potentially dangerous output and material you can genuinely use for what you're trying to do.

First, get your bearings in the names

For a beginner, navigating model names is often a bigger obstacle than choosing the tool itself. Here is where things stand today.

Claude (Anthropic)

On the Pro plan (USD 20 per month) the default model is Sonnet 5 as the fast workhorse. The strongest available model is Opus 5, suited to deep analysis and demanding texts. Above this line sit the Mythos/Fable-class models. Mythos itself is not publicly available. Fable 5 is accessible via API (e.g. the Claude platform or Vertex AI), or through direct use in the regular interface. For ordinary business work, though, you'll manage fine without Fable 5.

Gemini (Google)

You don't pick a numbered model but a working mode: Flash for everyday operational queries and Thinking for complex analytical tasks. Underneath runs the current Gemini generation. Across all plans this currently defaults to Gemini 3.6, or otherwise Gemini 3.1 Pro. A release of Gemini 3.6 Pro, or possibly Gemini 4.0, is expected around the turn of 2026/2027.

ChatGPT (OpenAI)

Here too you no longer pick a model but a level of reasoning. The default is Instant (powered by GPT-5.5 Instant) for fast answers, with Medium, High and Extra High above it, all running on GPT-5.6 Sol for demanding logical tasks. Labels such as Terra or Luna appear only in the API and enterprise environments.

Practical advice. For everyday matters, don't agonise over versions — leave the default setting. Switch to the stronger analytical mode only when you're dealing with a contract review, a financial model or a strategic question. On this front, Gemini is, at least for now, the simplest of the three in my view: you'll use Flash about 80% of the time, Thinking another 19%, and in roughly 1% of cases you'll reach for 3.1 Pro.

Pricing: standalone vs. bundled

Standalone individual plans cost practically the same across all three players — around USD 20 per month (Claude Pro, ChatGPT Plus, Google AI Pro). If you're looking for the cheapest individual entry point, there are more limited basic tiers such as Google AI Plus or ChatGPT Go. Worth noting: Google's AI plans also include cloud storage in the price.

If you need more comprehensive services or run a team, however, the economics change. Gemini within Google Workspace makes the most sense here. Google no longer sells separate AI add-ons on its Workspace licences — Gemini is now folded directly into the Workspace plans. In AI terms, the "basic" Business Standard tier comes to roughly USD 14 per user per month on annual billing. For that price you get not just an AI assistant but a complete business infrastructure: email on your own domain, shared cloud storage, calendar and an office suite.

The crucial part: what happens to your data

This is where companies most often go wrong. Choosing the right security regime matters far more than whether a model scores two percent better on a logic benchmark.

Tool / plan Trained on your data? Legal protection (DPA) Suitability for company data
Claude Pro / Max Yes, unless you switch it off manually in settings. With training enabled, data retention also extends from 30 days to 5 years. No guaranteed DPA on a standard account. Only after disabling training and anonymising.
ChatGPT Plus / Pro Yes, unless you switch it off in settings (data controls). No guaranteed DPA on a standard account. Only after disabling training and anonymising.
Gemini — consumer plan Yes, and prompts may be read by human reviewers. No DPA in place. Training essentially can't be switched off. Anonymise.
Gemini via Google Workspace No. Training is excluded by default. A DPA (in the form of a CDPA including SCCs) and EU data residency can be configured in the admin console. Suitable for standard business use and work with sensitive data.

On EU data residency: with Google Workspace the EU guarantee applies primarily to stored data. The computational processing of a query itself may technically run across global infrastructure. So this is not one hundred percent "EU-only processing". From a compliance standpoint, though, it's a far more robust position than any consumer version. With Claude or ChatGPT, you'll need to pay for enterprise licences or use API access to get anywhere close.

The risk called shadow AI

The biggest risk for entrepreneurs and companies today isn't the absence of AI tools but so-called shadow AI. In good faith, employees make their work easier by uploading sensitive data, client information, internal contracts or financial statements into public models. At that moment, the company loses control over who might potentially gain access to that data in the future — developers and hackers alike. Beyond the serious reputational risk, this is also a serious GDPR breach, and potentially a breach of contractual clauses with your business partners.

The answer isn't a ban — people will work around it. The answer is to give them a company account so they have no reason to reach for a private solution, and to add clear rules on what belongs where. One tool that fits well into such a setup is NotebookLM.

NotebookLM: a closed environment for your documents

If you need to work with internal policies, hundreds of pages of PDFs or recordings from meetings, try Google's NotebookLM. It draws on the power of Gemini, but you work in a closed environment over sources you upload yourself. The model's hallucination rate is minimised, because it draws exclusively on your own materials — and it gives you a precise citation for every answer. For research and an internal knowledge base, it's currently the strongest tool in its category.

An important distinction: NotebookLM within Google Workspace falls under the corporate DPA, and your data isn't used for training — you can work with sensitive documents here. NotebookLM on a private Google account runs in a similar regime to consumer Gemini, i.e. without a DPA. The risk is significantly lower than with an ordinary chat, but it isn't zero.

Anonymisation is not the same as pseudonymisation

Many entrepreneurs believe that replacing a client's name with "Client A" ticks the GDPR box. It does not.

Pseudonymisation — not enough

"Client A, the operator of the only bakery in a village of six hundred inhabitants near Tišnov, is claiming a CZK 340,000 reduction in the contract price following the oven failure on 14 March 2025."

The name is gone, but the person remains easily identifiable. In legal terms this is still personal data.

Anonymisation — this is how

"Client A, the operator of the only bakery in a village of six hundred inhabitants near Tišnov, is claiming a CZK 340,000 reduction in the contract price following the equipment failure on 14 March 2025."

The unique contextual details are removed as well — the trade combined with the size and location of the village, the exact amount and the date. Only now is the link to a specific person broken.

True anonymisation means the data cannot be traced back to a specific person by any reasonably available means, including in combination with other information. For smaller municipalities and distinctive cases, the bar is considerably higher than it appears.

For total peace of mind: if you're working with sensitive data, try to combine anonymisation with using a company account where training is switched off by default.

Where each tool falls short

To be fair, every system has its flaws. The following observations come from my own practice rather than public benchmarks — someone else's experience may look different.

  • Gemini: in my experience, somewhat weaker polish in written Czech; with stylised PR copy it can sound stilted and "translated". It also lags behind on more complex, multi-step reasoning. For ordinary business use, though, it's more than sufficient — its biggest strengths are the best-value bundle of services and native integration across the ecosystem.
  • Claude: slower, the free tier hits limits within a handful of queries, the interface is English-only, and it can't process audio. Of the three, though, it offers what I'd call the highest natural level of professional-grade output.
  • ChatGPT: built as a universal generalist — a Swiss Army knife. It may not be the outright leader in any single discipline, and on the free plan it can also bother you with advertising and strict limits. Its strengths are the sheer breadth of connectivity with external apps, speed, and generally solid output quality across the board.

Which tool for which task

Type of task Recommended tool Why
Contracts, legal analyses, long texts Claude Best handling of detail, precise style, consistency across a long document.
Everyday work in email, Docs and Sheets Gemini Direct integration into Google Workspace without having to copy or upload anything.
Research across your own documents and PDFs with citations NotebookLM A closed environment over your own sources with accurate referencing.
Audio and video analysis Gemini Native multimodality — of the three, it understands sound and image best.
Voice conversation ChatGPT The most developed ecosystem for voice input.
Image and video generation Gemini Its "nano banana" is already legendary.

My verdict for freelancers and small businesses

If, as an individual, you are primarily looking for a tool for writing, ideation and analysis of complex documents, invest in Claude Pro.

If your company already uses, or is considering, Google Workspace, the most sensible step is to start making full use of Gemini for Workspace. You get a solid assistant directly in your emails and documents, an environment without data training, and DPA coverage included in the price. For more demanding analytical tasks, I'd recommend adding a Claude Pro licence for key people — but use it strictly after anonymising your data.
