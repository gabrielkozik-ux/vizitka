AI Philosophy in Law: Why Not to Fear Synergy

Published: March 12, 2026 | Author: Mgr. Gabriel Kožík

People often ask me if I am not afraid that one day an algorithm will replace me as a lawyer. I answer no. Machines do not have empathy or strategic thinking, which are critical for solving complex legal disputes. Ever since I started experimenting with the first chatbots in December 2022 and later (from February 2024) began using Google AI Studio and Gemini daily, my work has changed fundamentally. I don't work less – I work differently and with much more depth.

Interestingly, the greatest strength of AI does not lie in writing contracts, but in interpretation and finding connections. I do not use technology as automatic text generators, but as sparring partners to analyze complicated legal issues.

Why Synergy?

For example, when I needed to map the case law regarding a managing director's duty to fulfill information obligations to shareholders upon request, I used Codexis AI. I needed to verify some of my conclusions. I was looking for specific statutory limits—namely, under what circumstances and in what manner a managing director can refuse to provide information in compliance with the Act on Business Corporations. Within minutes, Codexis AI identified the relevant Supreme Court decisions, analyzed the court's reasoning, and clearly summarized the boundaries for both parties. As a lawyer, searching through dozens of rulings would have taken me up to hours. Instead, I could immediately invest the saved time into formulating concrete recommendations for my client.

My philosophy of AI integration is built on several principles. First and foremost, it is about understanding the client's context, not mechanically copying paragraphs. AI allows me to go below the surface and find connections that might escape the human eye under time pressure. Furthermore, it is about dealing with routine work effectively and eliminating administrative errors. However, the most important point is the Human-in-the-loop approach – I personally review and complement every sentence the system proposes with my expertise. I stand behind the output, not the machine.

In most recent time, I work intensively with Google's agentic system, Antigravity, to optimize processes during web and presentation creation. Although I do not use these advanced agents directly in the legal field, they show me the direction in which efficient project management is heading.

The barrier between technology and traditional law practice has fallen. Anyone who does not understand this and continues to spend dozens of hours mechanically rewriting templates will soon lose competitiveness in the modern business market. Artificial intelligence will not replace lawyers, but lawyers with AI will replace those without it.
